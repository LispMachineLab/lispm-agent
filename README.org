#+title: lispm-emacs

LispM's Emacs distribution: Emacs set up as a workspace for coding agents, ready from the first
start.  [[https://github.com/RyanKung/lispm][LispM]] runs agents inside Emacs; lispm-emacs gives them a
place to work, and puts Claude Code, Codex and Hermes beside them when you have those installed.

* What you get

The frame is laid out as Mac OS 9 laid out its desktop, in the forms of its Platinum appearance.
Two appearances come with it, asked for at the first start and switched from the λ menu: Dracula,
and Platinum, Mac OS 9's own greys with black on white and the Lavender accent.

- *A desktop*, where Emacs starts, in the theme's colours tinted with its accent (Mac OS 8's
  blue-violet under Platinum).  Its icons stand down the right edge as Mac OS 9's did: the home
  disk and the applications, Emacs (with Emacs's own icon) and the browser, at the top, the
  Trash in the bottom corner.  Click one to select it, double-click it (or press Return) to
  open it.
- *A Finder window*, opened by the home disk: a folder's items as large icons, floating over
  the desktop, with back, forward, the enclosing folder and New Folder in its toolbar and the
  items and the room left counted at its bottom.  A folder opens in the window, a file in the
  editor, which comes forward.  The arrows select, Return opens, Command-Up, Command-[ and
  Command-] go up, back and forward, Command-Delete moves the selected item to the Trash (the
  system's own, from where it can be put back), typing a name's first letters selects it, and
  a right click offers Open, New Folder, Rename, Move to Trash and Copy Path.  It belongs to the desktop: it shows
  with it, and over the floating editor, and steps aside for the full-screen editor and the
  browser.
- *Applications*, one in front at a time, as on Mac OS 9: the desktop, Emacs the editor, and the
  browser.  The one in front fills the menu bar with its menus; the Application menu at the
  right end names it and lists the others.  Files, folders, terminals and agents opened while the
  desktop or the browser is in front open in the editor, which comes forward.
- *A menu bar* along the top: LispM's rainbow λ at its left end, as Mac OS 9 had its rainbow
  Apple, opening the λ menu (new agents, sessions, shells, the browser, the appearance), then the
  menus of the application in front: the desktop's File, Go and Special (Restart, Shut Down), the
  editor's File, Edit, View, Project and Window, the browser's File, Go and Window.  At its right
  end, Emacs's own load average, mail flag, battery and time, each a title with a menu of its
  own (the Mail menu reads, composes and checks mail), then the Application menu.
- *The editor*, an application of its own: a title strip across its top, the editor's title bar
  over a row of tabs choosing the sidebar's view (Files, Agents, Shells), then the sidebar and
  the windows.  Its workspaces, one per project, drop from its Project menu as a panel of
  thumbnails, each drawn from the workspace's windows and their text in its colours; C-Tab
  and C-Shift-Tab step through them, Command-` brings the panel up.  It runs full screen,
  under the menu bar, or floating in a window over the desktop, moved by its title bar, resized
  from its grow box and rolled up by its collapse box; its zoom box moves it between the two,
  workspaces and all.  Its close box puts it away, leaving the desktop; it opens again as it was.
- *A buffer manager*, from the editor's Window menu: every open buffer with what it costs, as
  Mac OS 9's About This Computer barred each application's memory: its kind and the size of its
  text, and for one running a process (a terminal, an agent's CLI) the CPU and memory of that
  process and every process under it.  It refreshes itself while shown; Return shows a buffer,
  k closes it.
- *A title bar on every window*: a close box, the title between pinstripes, a zoom box and a
  collapse box.  Only the active window shows stripes and boxes, and a box is pressed, not
  clicked: it sinks while held and acts when let go over it.  The close box puts the window's
  buffer away (the last one leaves the desktop's colour showing through), the zoom box gives the
  window the whole main area and back, and the collapse box rolls it up to its title bar.
  Double-click a title bar to roll the window up (or zoom one alone in its column); drag it onto
  another window to swap the two (sessions only with sessions); Command-click it for the
  workspace's buffers.
- *Scroll bars*: Mac OS 9's, down each window's right edge: arrow boxes at both ends, a recessed
  track and a thumb in the accent colour with grip ridges.  Arrows and track repeat while held,
  the thumb drags.  An inactive window shows its empty track; sessions scroll their own way.
- *Window frames*: neighbouring windows meet as Mac OS 9's windows were framed, a dark outline
  on each edge and platinum between.
- *A bottom edge on every window*: placards for the buffer, its position, its diagnostics, its
  branch and its mode, and the grow box in the active window's corner.
- *Finder's icons*: folders (badged when they hold a git repository), a hard disk, documents by
  kind (text, code, Lisp, Markdown, Org, picture), a compact Macintosh for terminals, a robot for
  the agents and LispM's diamond for its records, in the sidebar, the title bars, the Application
  menu and the project tree.
- *A sidebar laid out as a Finder list*: a header holding the view's bevel buttons, column
  headings, disclosure triangles, one row per item with its date or place in the right-hand
  column, shaded down its length as the sorted column was, the selected row in the highlight
  colour, and a count of the items at the bottom.  The agents view lists every agent session,
  running or recorded, grouped by project: LispM's, and those of Claude Code, Codex and Hermes
  when their CLI is on ~PATH~.  Click one to resume it where it ran.
- *A browser*, when Emacs is built with xwidgets: an application of its own, opened from the
  desktop or the λ menu and brought forward from the Application menu; the floating editor
  steps aside while it is in front.  Its window is a Mac OS 9 browser window: a title bar naming
  the page, a toolbar of bevel buttons, the address in an edit field and a row of tabs.
- *Menus and alerts*: the λ menu, the Application menu, a title bar's buffer menu, every
  right-click menu (Emacs's context menu, the sidebar's, the project tree's) and every menu a
  package pops up drop as Mac OS 9's menus did, with check marks, separators, key equivalents
  and hierarchical submenus; closing a modified document asks to save it (Return saves, Escape
  cancels, Command-D doesn't save), closing a running session or deleting one asks first, each
  in an alert with the caution, stop or note icon and the default button ringed.
- *Details*: a thin insertion point (Evil's normal state keeps its block), and help over a
  control shown as Balloon Help.
- *A main area* that keeps agent chats and terminals on the left and everything else, files
  first, on the right, however a buffer is opened, and *a workspace per project*, owning that
  project's buffers.
- *Agents that see Emacs*: Claude Code connects over its IDE protocol, Codex and Hermes over MCP,
  and all of them can read the selection, the open and unsaved files, and Flymake's diagnostics.
- *Completion everywhere* (Vertico, Orderless, Consult, Embark, Corfu), Magit, Eglot and
  tree-sitter modes.
- *Vim or Emacs keys*, chosen at the first start.  With vim keys, Evil edits program, text and
  configuration files, SPC is the leader key, and terminals, chats and the sidebar keep Emacs keys
  so the programs in them get every key.

Title bars and menus are set in Charcoal when it is installed, else Chicago (ChicagoFLF, a free
revival whose author considers it public domain, works too) or Geneva; lists in Geneva.  macOS
still ships Geneva; the others are not distributed with lispm-emacs.

* Installation

Requires Emacs 30.1 or later (31 recommended), git and curl, on macOS or GNU/Linux.

Try it without touching your configuration:

#+begin_src sh
git clone https://github.com/RyanKung/lispm-emacs ~/lispm-emacs
emacs --init-directory ~/lispm-emacs
#+end_src

or make it your configuration (move an existing =~/.emacs.d= and =~/.emacs= out of the way first):

#+begin_src sh
git clone https://github.com/RyanKung/lispm-emacs ~/.emacs.d
emacs
#+end_src

The first start asks whether you edit with vim or Emacs keys and which appearance you want,
installs the packages (a few minutes, once), and offers to install the icon font agents' marks
are drawn with.

Then give LispM a model: with ~OPENAI_API_KEY~ or ~ANTHROPIC_API_KEY~ set in Emacs's environment
nothing more is needed; for any other server, local or hosted, see LispM's
[[https://github.com/RyanKung/lispm#backends][Backends]] and put the settings in your =personal.el=.

Optional, and picked up when present:

- [[https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code][Claude Code]] (~claude~), Codex (~codex~) and Hermes
  (~hermes~) on ~PATH~;
- the Maple Mono NF CN font, whose glyphs keep terminal columns aligned, CJK included
  (=brew install --cask font-maple-mono-nf-cn= on macOS);
- a spell checker (aspell, hunspell or ispell) for Flyspell;
- an Emacs built with xwidgets, for the embedded browser.

* Keys

| Key                    | Does                                                       |
|------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------|
| =C-c a l=              | start a LispM agent in this project (=C-u= to pick a role) |
| =C-c a c=, =x=, =h=    | start Claude Code, Codex or Hermes in this project         |
| =C-c a s=              | resume any recorded session                                |
| =C-c a a=              | show or hide the agents view (or click Agents)             |
| =C-c a r=              | put a reference to the region into a running agent's input |
| =C-c t=                | a new terminal                                             |
| =C-<tab>=, =C-S-<tab>= | the next, the previous workspace of the editor             |
| =M-`=, =C-c w=         | the workspaces as thumbnails (Tab moves, Return opens)     |
| =C-c d=                | Dirvish, the file manager                                  |
| =C-x b=, =C-s=         | switch buffer, search the buffer (Consult)                 |
| =C-.=                  | act on the thing at point (Embark)                         |

On macOS the Command key is Meta, so Command-C and Command-V copy and paste everywhere, terminals
included.  With vim keys, =SPC= opens the leader: =SPC e= find a file, =SPC b= switch buffer,
=SPC g= Magit, =SPC t= a terminal, =SPC f r= ripgrep, and =which-key= shows the rest.

* Your own settings

Put them in =personal.el= next to =init.el= (git ignores it); it is loaded after lispm-emacs is
set up, so it can change anything.  To pick the other editing style later, customize
~lme-editing-style~; to switch appearance, use the λ menu's Appearance submenu or
=M-x lme-set-theme=.

An existing configuration can use lispm-emacs as a library instead:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(add-to-list 'load-path "~/src/lispm-emacs/lisp")
(setq lme-editing-style 'vim)           ; or 'emacs; nil asks
(require 'lme)
(lme-setup)
;; ... your settings
#+end_src

LispM comes from a checkout on ~load-path~ when there is one, and is otherwise installed from
GitHub.

* Updating

#+begin_src sh
git -C ~/.emacs.d pull
#+end_src

then =M-x package-upgrade-all= for the packages and =M-x package-vc-upgrade= for LispM.

* Tests

=make test= runs the ERT tests in batch; =make compile= byte-compiles every module against the
installed packages, to check for warnings, and leaves no =.elc= files behind.
